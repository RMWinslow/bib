### Zotero note

#### Simple 2x2 Homework Example

-   Suppose:

    -   Alice has 100 apples today and nothing tomorrow.

    -   Bob has nothing today but will have *either*:

        -   150 bananas tomorrow if the weather is bad,
        -   or 600 bananas tomorrow if the weather is good.

-   Here’s a trade that could make both people better off:

    -   Alice gives Bob 50 apples today in exchange for half his bananas tomorrow.
    -   If the weather is bad, this means Bob will give Alice 75 bananas.
    -   If the weather is good, this means he will give Alice 300 bananas.

-   The ex-post efficiency of this trade does *not* depend on how likely the weather is to be good.

    -   (To get this exact trade as optimal, assume utility is separable and logarithmic in each period/state.)

-   This trade is also *ex-ante* efficient if Alice and Bob have the same beliefs about tomorrow’s weather.

    -   This means there is no way for them both to increase their *expected* utility by changing the terms.
    -   The result still holds if Alice and Bob have the *same incorrect beliefs* about the future.

-   But now suppose Alice is more optimistic than Bob is.

    -   Alice believes that Bob has a 2 / 3 chance of having good weather tomorrow.
    -   Bob believes there’s only has a 1 / 3 chance of good weather tomorrow.

-   With the same utility as above, the ex-ante efficient contract is now:

    -   Alice gives Bob 50 apples today, same as before.

    -   If the weather is good, Bob gives Alice 400 bananas, keeping 200 for himself.

    -   If the weather is bad, Bob gives Alice 50 bananas, keeping 100 for himself.

    -   Compared to the ex-post efficient trade, where they always split the fruit evenly:

        -   Alice the optimist gets more fruit if the weather is good, and
        -   Bob the pessimist gets more fruit if the weather is bad.

    -   (Furthermore, the relative welfare of the two now depends on whose beliefs were closer to the truth.)

-   Verifying the above trades are efficient is left as an exercise to the reader.

    -   The obvious analogies are also left as an exercise to the reader.
